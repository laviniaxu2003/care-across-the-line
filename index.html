Local resources

Where do reproductive healthcare barriers overlap?

The largest increases in distance landed where local care was already thinnest: 589 counties gained 100 miles or more, and 316 of them had no OB-GYN at all.

Restrictive states have more nonmetro counties, and nonmetro counties have fewer OB-GYNs. The gap survives that comparison.

And for a quarter of the country, the nearest clinic is in another state altogether.

Across state lines

What about care across state lines?

16.7 million women aged 15–44 live in counties whose nearest open abortion clinic is in another state.

A state’s policy decides where clinics operate inside its borders, not where its residents’ nearest clinic sits.

What follows

What this means for planning

These data show where the nearest clinic is and what care exists nearby. Three things follow.

  • 01Plan across state lines, not inside them. For 16.7 million women aged 15–44 the nearest clinic is in another state, so a policy map does not describe where people would have to go.
  • 02Where abortion care is now far away, maternity care has to be close. 316 counties that gained 100+ miles had no OB-GYN, and 297 of those no obstetric hospital either. Whatever one thinks about abortion policy, those counties need prenatal and delivery care within reach.
  • 03Read these as potential access, not as care received. Distance and clinic counts say nothing about appointments, staffing, or who actually travels, and should be paired with capacity and utilization data before any allocation decision.
