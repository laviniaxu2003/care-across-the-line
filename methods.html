How the catchment is calculated

For each of the 3,143 US counties, the Myers database gives the road distance to the nearest facility providing abortion and the state that facility sits in. Rolling those counties up by home state → nearest-clinic state produces the cross-border flows drawn on the map. A state’s load is the number of women aged 15–44 whose nearest clinic falls inside it, divided by the number of clinics it has open. That count includes its own residents and everyone arriving from outside.

What these numbers do not say

  • 01Distance is exposure, not travel. We measure how far the nearest clinic is, not where anyone actually goes. Telehealth, shield laws, money, time off work and childcare all change real journeys, and none of them appear here.
  • 02Policy is a state attribute. Every county in Texas carries the same policy tier. Counties differ from one another on distance, on provider supply and on preventive care. They never differ on law.
  • 03Preventive care figures are modelled. CDC PLACES publishes small-area estimates, not counts. They are sound for comparing places and wrong to quote as a precise local value.
  • 04We make no causal claim about health outcomes. The gap in infant mortality between restrictive and protective states is large, but it predates Dobbs and has not meaningfully widened in the two years of data since. We say that plainly rather than imply otherwise.
