Visualization drafts

Two designs from the Milestone 4 selection that we have not implemented. Both are drawn here from the project’s own derived tables rather than mocked up, so the numbers are the ones we would ship. Sketches 10 and 19 are already live in the county explorer.

Sketch 13 · redesigned

A walk through eight years

The original sketch used infant footprints to carry two rates at once. The metaphor survives the move to screen if the footprint stops being decoration and becomes the mark itself: one print per group per year, its area set by the rate, the stride alternating so the row reads as a gait rather than a bar chart lying on its side. Time runs left to right, and the 2020 teen birth rate sits in its own column at the right because it is a single cross-section, not a trend.

What it shows. Both groups improved in parallel, and the distance between them barely moved: a gap of 1.69 deaths per 1,000 in 2017 and 1.70 in 2024. The teen birth column is the harder number. In 2020, two years before the ruling, the most restrictive states already stood at 20.8 per 1,000 against 11.6 in the most protective.

Sketch 15 · redesigned

The band that did not tighten

The original sketch read as a tourniquet, a compression band narrowing around a limb. Translated to data, each policy group becomes a ribbon whose thickness is its preterm birth rate, and the strap sits where the ruling falls. The design was built to make a post-Dobbs cinch visible. Below the ribbons, the panel plots the gap the metaphor predicts, which is the only place the question can actually be answered.

What it shows, and why we would still build it. The ribbons do not pinch. The gap runs 1.60 points in 2017, peaks at 1.92 in 2021, and sits at 1.77 in 2024, so the widest separation on record arrives a year before the ruling. A design that sets out to reveal an effect and then reports its absence is doing its job. This is the figure that stops the project claiming a health shock the data cannot support.
